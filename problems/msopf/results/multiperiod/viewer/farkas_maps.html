Certified Feasibility Maps

Which upstream angle differences can the feeder live with? At equal angles (the centre) it works. A direction is one fixed recipe for tilting the angles apart, for example "raise substation 2 only" or "raise 2 and lower 3 equally"; along it we push the tilt further until no valid dispatch exists. Two substations have one angle difference, so just 2 directions (+ and −); three have a plane, sampled every 22.5° (16 directions); four have a 3-D space, sampled by 78 directions spread evenly over a sphere like golf-ball dimples. Each system has four substations, and every pair (6) and triple (4) of them is studied as well. Each direction is bracketed from both sides (Farkas bracketing), in the paper's colours. Batteries idle: green, a validated AC dispatch at 98% of the limiting hour's edge; crimson, the proven edge, beyond which a sparse semidefinite relaxation proves no dispatch exists. Batteries used over the day (cyclic state of charge), where that campaign has run: light blue, the validated reach; blue, its proven edge from one relaxation of the whole day. The OPF solves model no current limits; the certificates hold for the model with empirical current limits (the largest currents of the validated dispatches).
