## IEEE2522C feasibility gate

The corrected active feeder name is `ieee2522C_1ph`; the former
`ieee2552C_1ph` name was a typo. The initial loss-aware centralized tests were
infeasible because the converted feeder retained aggregate three-phase powers
but divided the 7.2-kV voltage base by $\sqrt{3}$, while the OPF parser used
2.4018 kV and effectively divided it again. This made the per-unit line
impedances nine times too large.

- `T = 3`: Ipopt terminated `LOCALLY_INFEASIBLE` after 363 iterations. The
  final constraint violation was `3.349e-02` p.u.; the validator reported 1526
  violations (1461 real-power-balance and 65 voltage-drop violations).
- `T = 6`: Ipopt terminated `LOCALLY_INFEASIBLE` after 447 iterations. The
  final constraint violation was `3.462e-02` p.u.; the validator reported 3349
  violations (3213 real-power-balance and 136 voltage-drop violations).

The exact paper constraint was then supplied to Gurobi as the equivalent native
cone `norm([2P,2Q,v-ell]) <= v+ell`, without a warm start. Gurobi globally
declared both models infeasible: `T = 3` in 8.6 s and `T = 6` in 18.0 s. Thus
the Ipopt behavior was not merely a local-solver artifact.

A `T = 3` LinDistFlow diagnostic was also globally infeasible with the original
0.95--1.05 p.u. voltage band. Removing only those voltage bounds made the
remaining linear model optimal; every non-voltage equation validated, while
all 7,515 omitted voltage-bound checks failed and the maximum squared-voltage
violation was 1.73 p.u. The contradiction is therefore in the combination of
the parsed voltage-drop model and the universal voltage band, not in warm
starting, temporal battery coupling, line-loss terms, or the SOC encoding.
These results are retained as diagnosis of the superseded, incorrectly scaled
case. The active one-phase aggregate equivalent now uses 7.2 kV and 1 MVA,
giving $Z_{\rm base}=51.84\ \Omega$; aggregate powers and physical line
impedances were not changed. With the original 0.95--1.05 p.u. voltage band,
no warm start, full loss terms, and native Gurobi second-order cones, the
corrected model is globally optimal and independently validated from `T = 3`
through `T = 96`. Every branch-flow, voltage, device, storage, and SOC
constraint passed the post-solve validator. Cold-start Gurobi results are:

- `T = 3`: objective $8515.8813; Gurobi 1.730 s; solve wall 3.507 s.
- `T = 6`: objective $9040.0153; Gurobi 5.334 s; solve wall 7.374 s.
- `T = 12`: objective $8512.5167; Gurobi 11.015 s; solve wall 14.705 s.
- `T = 24`: objective $8632.2771; Gurobi 27.224 s; solve wall 31.611 s.
- `T = 48`: objective $8701.1489; Gurobi 58.151 s; solve wall 65.543 s.
- `T = 96`: objective $8737.6487; Gurobi 121.305 s; solve wall 135.476 s.

The machine-readable record is
`ieee2522c_corrected_gurobi_sweep.csv`. Objectives across different horizons
are not expected to be identical because the deterministic load, PV, and price
curves are resampled at each `T`; the low-resolution `T = 3` and `T = 6`
profiles in particular sample different operating points.

### Original dense FilterDDP scalability attempt

The corrected `T = 3` data were exported with the 7.2-kV aggregate base and
transcribed into the same loss-aware FilterDDP model used for IEEE123C. Its
per-stage dimensions are `nx = 250`, `nu = 13,358`, and `nc = 10,337`; the
dense control Hessian contains 178,436,164 entries and the null-space reduced
dimension is 3,021.

One remaining wrapper-level `SVector{nu}` conversion was removed, reducing
model construction from a multi-minute compilation stall to 2.33 s. Dynamic
solver storage then allocated in 1.41 s. A guarded one-iteration run initially
failed on an unnecessary dense copy in `H + 0I`; the local FilterDDP fork was
changed to add nonzero regularization in place. After that change, iteration 1
still did not complete: the package forms a dense QR factorization of the
13,358-by-10,337 equality Jacobian and explicitly materializes its full
13,358-by-13,358 `Q`. The run was stopped during that first stage factorization.

Thus FilterDDP completed zero iterations and made zero Gurobi/Ipopt calls; its
external-solver time is exactly zero, not an unavailable measurement. The
centralized `T = 3` native-conic reference remains objective $8515.8813,
Gurobi time 1.730 s, and 22 barrier iterations. There are no meaningful
per-time-step Gurobi/Ipopt times for either method: the centralized reference
is one monolithic Gurobi solve, while FilterDDP uses its own dense linear
algebra at each stage. `T > 3` was not launched because it has the same
per-stage dense factorization and proportionally more stages. A sparse or
structure-exploiting null-space implementation is required before a genuine
IEEE2522C FilterDDP timing sweep is possible. The machine-readable attempt
record is `ieee2522c_filterddp_attempts.csv`.

### Sparse-KKT FilterDDP result

The dense architectural limit above was subsequently removed. Network
derivative callbacks now emit sparse Jacobians and local Hessian blocks, and
the backward pass solves the sparse saddle-point system directly instead of
forming a dense QR null-space basis and `Z' * H * Z`. The existing dense path
remains available for ordinary compact models.

On the same corrected IEEE2522C `T = 3` instance, sparse FilterDDP completed
56 iterations in 233.098 s, with objective 8515.880516246880 and maximum
equality residual `5.631e-10`. The independently stored centralized reference
has objective 8515.881250487677, giving an absolute gap of `7.342e-4`; the
largest battery-energy difference was `1.439e-3` p.u.h. This converts the old
zero-iteration failure into a verified solve, though generic sparse LU remains
far slower than the 1.730-s native-conic Gurobi reference.

Cross-checks used the same sparse path. IEEE123C `T = 3` converged in 48
iterations and 13.856 s with objective gap `1.193e-4`; ADS10 `T = 3` converged
in 39 iterations and 10.191 s. ADS10's larger objective gap (`5.470e-2`) and
line-current discrepancy reflect weakly penalized slack/current degeneracy;
IEEE123C and IEEE2522C agree closely in all reported physical variables. The
machine-readable record is `sparse_kkt_filterddp_sweep.csv`.

Without a warm start, IEEE2522C `T = 6` converged in 82 FilterDDP iterations
and 836.933 s. Its objective gap against the stored centralized reference was
`1.305e-3`, the maximum equality residual was `1.753e-9`, and the largest
reported physical-variable difference was `9.716e-4`.

IEEE2522C `T = 12`, again without a warm start, converged in 79 iterations
and 1902.103 s (31.70 min). Its objective gap was `9.207e-5`, the maximum
equality residual was `2.290e-8`, and the largest reported physical-variable
difference was `4.382e-4`.

A symbolic-factorization-reuse experiment was rejected: rerunning the identical
`T = 3` case took 295.296 s instead of 233.098 s, with the same 56 iterations
and solution. Generic UMFPACK symbolic analysis is therefore not the dominant
cost here; retaining that cache would make the implementation more complicated
and slower. Further scaling requires reducing numerical factorization/solve
cost or exploiting feeder structure, not merely caching the ordering.

## Large10kC feasibility gate

The original `large10kC_1ph` conversion was globally infeasible even at `T=1`.
It declared a 12.47-kV system but wrote 7.1996 kV into the one-phase aggregate
source and devices, while the OPF parser used 2.4018 kV. Correcting the
aggregate base to 12.47 kV was necessary but not sufficient. A bounded
LinDistFlow diagnostic remained infeasible; without voltage bounds its minimum
voltage was 0.762 p.u., localizing the remaining defect to the synthetic feeder
design rather than the loss or SOC equations.

The synthetic network originally used `(r1,x1)=(0.07,0.01)` ohm for all 10,219
ordinary line sections. Half those impedances still failed the 0.95-p.u. lower
bound, whereas quarter-length sections passed it. The active feeder now uses
`(0.0175,0.0025)` ohm on ordinary sections and
`(0.000025,0.000025)` ohm on its 101 area-boundary sections. Powers, DER
capacities, the 0.95--1.05 p.u. band, all loss terms, and the exact SOC were
unchanged. The data-side rationale is preserved in
`rawData/large10kC_1ph/CORRECTION.md`.

The permanently corrected files were then solved again without electrical
runtime overrides or warm starts. OpenDSS converged with 681.406 MW load,
685.787 MW substation supply, and 4.381 MW losses. Native-conic Gurobi returned
global optima and every constraint passed the independent validator:

- `T = 1`: 44,345 variables, 66,007 constraints, objective $3,322,860.7569,
  Gurobi 2.413 s, solve wall 3.660 s.
- `T = 3`: 133,035 variables, 198,021 constraints, objective $2,985,281.3189,
  Gurobi 6.304 s, solve wall 8.241 s.

The machine-readable record is `large10kc_corrected_gurobi_start.csv`.
