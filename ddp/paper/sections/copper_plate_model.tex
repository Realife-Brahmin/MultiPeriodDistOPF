\section{Copper-Plate Storage-Scheduling Model}
\label{sec:copper_plate}

\subsection{Problem Statement}

We consider the smallest multi-period scheduling problem that still exhibits the
two features a differential dynamic programming (DDP) solver must contend with in
an MPOPF setting: an inter-temporal state coupled by a discrete-time dynamic, and
a nonlinear equality constraint that must hold in every period.  The network is
collapsed to a single node---a copper plate---so that the model retains the
temporal structure of the MPOPF of~\cite{Jha2025} while carrying none of its
spatial structure.

A single storage device and a single grid connection serve a known demand
$p_L^t$ over a horizon of $T$ periods of length $\Delta t$.  Following the sign
convention of the branch-flow formulation, $P_B^t > 0$ denotes discharging and
$P_B^t < 0$ charging.  All quantities are in per unit on a common power base.

\smallskip
\noindent\textit{Remark on indexing.}\quad
Unlike the end-of-period convention used for $B_j^t$ in the tADMM
formulation, $B^t$ here denotes the energy at the \emph{start} of period $t$, so
that $B^1$ is given and $B^{T+1}$ is the terminal energy.  This is required by
the optimal-control form of Section~\ref{subsec:ocp}, in which the state entering
a stage must be the quantity the stage acts upon.

\subsection{Optimization Problem}

The complete problem is stated in one place below; the paragraphs that follow
comment on its parts but do not restate them.  The decision variables are the
grid import $P_{\text{Subs}}^t$, the storage power $P_B^t$ and the stored energy
$B^t$, over $t \in \mathcal{T} = \{1,\dots,T\}$, with $B^{T+1}$ the terminal
energy:
\begin{subequations}
\label{eq:cp_all}
\begin{alignat}{2}
\min_{P_{\text{Subs}},\,P_B,\,B} \;\;
  & \sum_{t=1}^{T} \Bigl[ c^t P_{\text{Subs}}^t
                        + C_B \bigl(P_B^t\bigr)^2 \Bigr] \Delta t \nonumber \\[-2pt]
  & \hspace{34pt} +\; w \bigl(B^{T+1} - B^1\bigr)^2
\label{eq:cp_obj} \\[2pt]
\text{s.t.} \;\;
  & P_{\text{Subs}}^t + P_B^t - p_L^t = 0, &\quad& t \in \mathcal{T}
\label{eq:cp_balance} \\
  & B^1 = B_0
\label{eq:cp_soc_init} \\
  & B^{t+1} = B^t - P_B^t \Delta t, && t \in \mathcal{T}
\label{eq:cp_soc_dyn} \\
  & P_{\text{Subs}}^t \geq 0, && t \in \mathcal{T}
\label{eq:cp_pg_lim} \\
  & P_B^{\min} \leq P_B^t \leq P_B^{\max}, && t \in \mathcal{T}
\label{eq:cp_pb_lim} \\
  & B^{\min} \leq B^{t+1} \leq B^{\max}, && t \in \mathcal{T}
\label{eq:cp_soc_lim}
\end{alignat}
\end{subequations}

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Objective~\eqref{eq:cp_obj}.}\quad
This is the objective of the tADMM formulation~\cite{Jha2025} unchanged: the cost
of energy drawn from the substation at the time-varying price $c^t$, plus a small
quadratic charge on battery throughput with coefficient $C_B$, whose role there is
to discourage unnecessary cycling.  Only the terminal term is new.  Note that no
quadratic term on $P_{\text{Subs}}^t$ is needed: $C_B > 0$ alone makes the
objective strictly convex on the feasible set, which is what renders the instance
solvable in closed form and therefore usable as a reference.  Concretely, with
$n_u = 2$ and $n_c = 1$ the balance Jacobian is $[1\;\;1]$, its null space is
spanned by $(1,-1)$, and the reduced Hessian along that direction evaluates to
$C_B$.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Power balance~\eqref{eq:cp_balance}.}\quad
With the network collapsed to a single node, the nodal balance of~\cite{Jha2025}
reduces to a single equality per period.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Storage~\eqref{eq:cp_soc_init}--\eqref{eq:cp_soc_dyn}.}\quad
A lossless energy reservoir, coupled sequentially in time.  This is the only
coupling between periods.  No round-trip efficiency term appears, and its
absence is deliberate: a single $\eta$ multiplying a signed $P_B^t$ cannot be
correct in both directions, since charging stores $\eta_c |P_B^t|$ whereas
discharging $P_B^t$ into the bus must drain $P_B^t/\eta_d$ from the device, and
$\eta$ and $1/\eta$ agree only at $\eta = 1$.  Representing losses honestly
requires split variables $P_c^t, P_d^t \geq 0$ with
$B^{t+1} = B^t + \eta_c P_c^t \Delta t - P_d^t \Delta t / \eta_d$, which is the
route taken if losses are wanted later (see the remark closing
Section~\ref{subsec:ocp}).  The tADMM
formulation of~\cite{Jha2025} models a lossless battery, so this benchmark does
the same and drops the symbol rather than carrying it at unity.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Operational limits~\eqref{eq:cp_pg_lim}--\eqref{eq:cp_soc_lim}.}\quad
Introduced one at a time.  Substation import carries no upper limit; as
in~\cite{Jha2025} it is only held non-negative, so that no energy is exported
upstream.  The \emph{base instance} is the equality-constrained
problem~\eqref{eq:cp_obj}--\eqref{eq:cp_soc_dyn} alone, with all three bounds
dropped; it is studied on its own before any inequality is added.
Fig.~\ref{fig:schedule} shows the instance solved with all three imposed, at
$T = 6$; recall that $P_B^t > 0$ discharges, so a negative bar there is the
battery charging.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{On the terminal energy.}\quad
The natural requirement that the device return to its initial state of charge,
$B^{T+1} = B^1$, is imposed here as the quadratic penalty in~\eqref{eq:cp_obj}
rather than as a hard equality.  The reason is a restriction of the solver rather
than of the model: the DDP formulation of Section~\ref{subsec:ocp} admits a
single stagewise constraint function shared by all stages, so a terminal-only
equality cannot be expressed.  The penalty is nevertheless \emph{exact} in the
variables it uses, since $B^{T+1} = B^T - P_B^T \Delta t$ is available from
the stage-$T$ state and control; the weight $w$ therefore trades terminal energy
against operating cost without introducing any approximation of the dynamics.

\subsection{Optimal-Control Form}
\label{subsec:ocp}

Problem~\eqref{eq:cp_obj}--\eqref{eq:cp_soc_dyn} is restated as a discrete-time
optimal control problem over $N = T$ stages, in the form required by DDP:
\begin{align}
\min_{x,u} \;\; & \sum_{t=1}^{N-1} \ell\bigl(x^t, u^t\bigr)
                  + \ell^{F}\bigl(x^N, u^N\bigr) \nonumber \\
\text{s.t.} \;\; & x^1 = \hat{x}^1, \label{eq:ocp_form} \\
                 & x^{t+1} = f\bigl(x^t, u^t\bigr),
                   \quad t \in \{1,\dots,N-1\}, \nonumber \\
                 & c\bigl(x^t, u^t\bigr) = 0,
                   \quad t \in \{1,\dots,N\}. \nonumber
\end{align}

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{States and controls.}\quad
The state carries the stored energy and an explicit time index $\tau$; the
control carries the two power set-points:
\begin{equation}
x^t = \bigl(B^t,\; \tau^t\bigr)^\top \in \mathbb{R}^2,
\qquad
u^t = \bigl(P_{\text{Subs}}^t,\; P_B^t\bigr)^\top \in \mathbb{R}^2 ,
\label{eq:ocp_xu}
\end{equation}
with $\hat{x}^1 = (B_0,\, 1)^\top$.

The time index is a state out of necessity, not modeling preference.  The DDP
formulation~\eqref{eq:ocp_form} admits one stage cost $\ell$ and one constraint
function $c$ for the entire horizon, so per-period data cannot be indexed and
must instead be recovered from the state.  We therefore carry $\tau$ and
represent the period-varying data by the Lagrange interpolants through the nodes
$\tau = 1,\dots,T$,
\begin{equation}
p_L(\tau) = \sum_{k=1}^{T} p_L^k \prod_{\substack{m=1 \\ m \neq k}}^{T}
             \frac{\tau - m}{k - m},
\label{eq:ocp_interp}
\end{equation}
and likewise for the price $c(\tau)$.  These are exact at every node and
infinitely differentiable, as DDP requires; their degree grows with $T$, which
bounds the horizons for which the device is practical.
Fig.~\ref{fig:input_curves} plots them against the data they interpolate.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Dynamics, costs and constraint.}\quad
\begin{align}
f(x, u) &= \bigl(\, x_1 - u_2 \Delta t,\;\; x_2 + 1 \,\bigr)^\top,
\label{eq:ocp_f} \\
c(x, u) &= u_1 + u_2 - p_L(x_2),
\label{eq:ocp_c} \\
\ell(x, u) &= \bigl[\, c(x_2)\, u_1 + C_B\, u_2^2 \,\bigr] \Delta t,
\label{eq:ocp_l} \\
\ell^{F}(x, u) &= \bigl[\, c^T u_1 + C_B\, u_2^2 \,\bigr] \Delta t \nonumber \\
               &\qquad + w \bigl( x_1 - u_2 \Delta t - B_0 \bigr)^2 .
\label{eq:ocp_lF}
\end{align}
The terminal cost~\eqref{eq:ocp_lF} depends on $u^N$ as well as $x^N$, which is
what allows the terminal energy $B^{T+1}$ to appear exactly.  The bounds
\eqref{eq:cp_pg_lim}--\eqref{eq:cp_pb_lim} map directly onto control limits
$b^{L} \leq u^t \leq b^{U}$.  The energy
bound~\eqref{eq:cp_soc_lim} is a state bound, which the formulation does not
admit; it is introduced by appending a slack $s^t$ to the control vector and
replacing~\eqref{eq:cp_soc_lim} with
\begin{equation}
\bigl(x_1 - u_2 \Delta t\bigr) - B^{\min} - s^t = 0,
\qquad 0 \leq s^t \leq B^{\max} - B^{\min},
\label{eq:ocp_slack}
\end{equation}
appended as a second row of $c$, so that $n_c = 2$ and $n_u = 3$.

\smallskip
\noindent\textit{Remark (adding conversion losses).}\quad
\label{rem:eta}
The split-variable storage model of Section~\ref{sec:copper_plate} maps into this
form without difficulty: $P_c^t, P_d^t \geq 0$ become two controls carrying native
lower bounds, the balance~\eqref{eq:cp_balance} becomes
$P_{\text{Subs}}^t + P_d^t - P_c^t - p_L^t = 0$, and the dynamics stay linear, so
$n_u = 3$ against $n_c = 1$ and the rank condition is comfortably met.  The
difficulty is elsewhere.  Non-simultaneity, $P_c^t P_d^t = 0$, is normally left
implicit on the grounds that with $\eta_c \eta_d < 1$ and a positive throughput
cost, charging and discharging at once is never optimal.  Imposing it explicitly
would make it a complementarity constraint, whose Jacobian loses rank exactly at
the solutions of interest --- precisely the degeneracy the backward pass cannot
absorb.  Conversion losses are therefore cheap to add to this model only for as
long as the implicit argument holds.

\subsection{Problem Characteristics}

The base instance is a strictly convex equality-constrained quadratic program:
$3T$ variables, $T$ balance equalities~\eqref{eq:cp_balance} and $T+1$ storage
equalities~\eqref{eq:cp_soc_init}--\eqref{eq:cp_soc_dyn}.  Eliminating
$P_{\text{Subs}}^t = p_L^t - P_B^t$ and
$B^{T+1} - B^1 = -\Delta t \sum_t P_B^t$ reduces it to the unconstrained
quadratic
\begin{align}
J(P_B) = \text{const} & - \Delta t \sum_t c^t P_B^t
       + C_B \Delta t \sum_t \bigl(P_B^t\bigr)^2 \nonumber \\
       & + w (\Delta t)^2 s^2 ,
\label{eq:cp_reduced}
\end{align}
with $s = \sum_t P_B^t$.  Dividing stationarity through by $\Delta t$ gives
$-c^k + 2 C_B P_B^k + 2 w \Delta t\, s = 0$, and summing over $k$ collapses
the system to a single scalar:
\begin{equation}
s = \frac{\sum_{t} c^t}{2 C_B + 2 T w \Delta t},
\qquad
P_B^k = \frac{c^k - 2 w \Delta t\, s}{2 C_B} .
\label{eq:cp_closed_form}
\end{equation}
The instance therefore admits a closed-form reference solution against which any
solver applied to it can be checked exactly, which is the reason for its
construction.

Storage~\eqref{eq:cp_soc_dyn} is the only coupling between periods; without the
terminal term in~\eqref{eq:cp_obj} the objective would not depend on $P_B$ at
all, and the problem would separate into $T$ independent single-period problems.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Reference implementation.}\quad
Problem~\eqref{eq:cp_all} is implemented in
\texttt{copper\_plate\_battery.jl} (base instance) and
\texttt{copper\_plate\_battery\_bounds.jl}
(with~\eqref{eq:cp_pg_lim}--\eqref{eq:cp_soc_lim}), both under
\texttt{ddp/examples/power\_system/}.
Both carry $\Delta t$ symbolically, so the implementation matches
\eqref{eq:cp_all} term for term.  One choice is worth flagging: $C_B$ is set to
$0.5$ here, whereas the tADMM paper uses $C_B \approx 10^{-6} \min_t c^t$,
deliberately near-zero so that battery throughput cannot compete with energy
cost.  Since $C_B$ is exactly the reduced-Hessian curvature, that near-zero value
makes the instance badly conditioned by construction; a healthy $C_B$ is used for
the base test, and shrinking it toward the paper's value is better treated as a
deliberate conditioning study than as the default.
